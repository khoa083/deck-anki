Phrasal verbs with get
There are many phrasal verbs with get, some of which you may have already met in this book 
or elsewhere. For example, get your act together [become more organised (Unit 8)], get 
around [travel, of news or rumours (Unit 9)], get by [have just enough money to pay for the 
things you need but no more (Unit 6)], get someone down [make someone depressed  
(Unit 10)] and get off [finish work (Unit 31)].

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- get across sth or get sth across — to successfully communicate an idea to other people (units 57)
- get around — if news or information gets around, a lot of people hear about it (units 9, 57)
- get around — to travel to a lot of different places (units 57)
- get away with sth/doing sth — to succeed in not being criticised or punished for something wrong that you have done (units 57)
- get away with murder — to succeed in not being criticised or punished for something wrong that you have done (units 57)
- get back into sth — to begin doing something again after not doing it for a period of time (units 57)
- get back to basics — to start again at the beginning (units 57)
- get by — to have just enough money to pay for the things that you need, but nothing more (units 6, 57)
- get down to sth/doing sth — to start doing something seriously and with a lot of your attention and effort (units 57)
- get sb down — to make someone feel unhappy (units 10, 57)
- get in sth or get sth in — to manage to do something even though you do not have much time because you are busy doing other things (units 57)
- get in on sth — informal to start to become involved in an activity that other people are already doing, often without being invited to (units 57)
- get into sth — to become interested in an activity or subject, or to start being involved in an activity (units 42, 57)
- get off — to leave the place where you work, usually at the end of the day (units 31, 57)
- get off sb’s back — to stop nagging someone (units 57)
- get off the ground — to get started (units 57)
- get sth out of sth/doing sth — to enjoy something or think that something is useful (units 57)
- get sth out of your mind — to stop thinking about something (units 57)
- get to the bottom of sth — to understand something properly, not superficially (units 57)
- get together — if two or more people get together, or if someone gets two or more people together, they meet in order to do something or spend time together (units 57)
- get up to sth — to do something, especially something that other people think is wrong (units 42, 57)
- get your act together — to organise yourself more efficiently (units 8, 57)
