What are phrasal nouns?
Like phrasal verbs, phrasal nouns consist of a verb combined with a particle. The particle may 
come before or aft er the verb.

meaning
example

Some such nouns have a corresponding phrasal verb, but some don’t. For example, there 
is no phrasal verb to kill over. The phrasal verb set on exists but it means attack. The verb 
related to onset is set in: We couldn’t continue playing aft er the rain set in.
If the particle is in first place, then the phrasal noun is never written with a hyphen. If 
the particle comes second, then there is sometimes a hyphen between the two parts of 
the phrasal noun, particularly if that particle is in or up or if the phrasal noun is relatively 
infrequent, e.g. walk-on [small part, with no words, in a play], stand-off .
When are phrasal nouns used?
Phrasal nouns are used frequently in newspapers and informal conversation.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- back-up — n support, help or something that you have arranged in case your main plans or equipment go wrong (units 3, 54)
- breakdown — n when talks between two groups of people fail because of a disagreement or problem (units 3)
- break-in — n when someone manages to get in a building by using force, usually in order to steal something (units 3)
- breakout — n when something dangerous and unpleasant, like war, disease or fire, suddenly starts (units 3)
- build up (sth) or build (sth) up — to increase in amount, size, or strength, or to make something increase in amount, size, or strength (units 3, 26)
- build-up — n when something increases in amount, size or strength (units 3)
- buy out sb/sth or buy sb/sth out — to buy part of a company or building that belonged to someone else so that you own all of it (units 3, 35)
- buyout — n the buying of a company, especially by the people who previously managed it or worked for it (units 3)
- comeback — n when a performer gives a performance he hopes will make him popular again (units 3)
- downpour — n a sudden, heavy fall of rain (units 3)
- input — n contribution to a system to help it operate (units 3)
- knockabout — n British informal when two or more people kick or hit a ball to each other for pleasure but not in a serious way, sometimes to warm up before a game (units 3)
- letdown — n something which is not as good as you thought it would be (units 3, 42)
- lift-off — n the moment when a spacecraft leaves the ground (units 3)
- lookout — n a person who looks at what is happening in the area around them, especially in order to watch for any danger (units 3)
- onset — n the moment at which something unpleasant starts (units 1, 3)
- outbreak — n a sudden beginning of something, especially something unpleasant (units 3)
- outlook — n the likely future situation (units 3)
- output — n an amount of something, produced by a person, machine, factory or country (units 3)
- overkill — n when there is more of something than is needed (units 3)
- set in — if something unpleasant sets in, it begins and seems likely to continue (units 3, 11)
- set (sth/sb) on sb — to attack someone, or to make a person or animal attack someone (units 3, 20)
- standby — n a person or thing that can be used if someone or something else is not available or cannot be used (units 1, 3)
- stand-off — n when two groups fail to reach an agreement in talks (units 3)
- start-up — n when a business or organisation is created and starts to operate (units 3)
- stow away — to hide on a ship, aircraft, or other vehicle, in order to travel secretly or without paying (units 3, 50)
- stowaway — n a person who stows away (units 3, 50)
- toss-up — n informal a situation in which two people or things seem equally likely to be chosen or two possible results seem equally likely to happen (units 3)
- walk-on — adj a walk-on part in a play is a very small part with no words for the actor to speak (units 3)
- walk out — to stop working because of a disagreement with your employer (units 3, 51)
- walkout — n when workers stop working because of a disagreement with their employer (units 3)
- warm-up — n when a performer makes a group of people who are going to watch a performance start to enjoy themselves by entertaining them for a short time before the performance (units 3)
