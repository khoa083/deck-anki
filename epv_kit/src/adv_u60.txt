Typical meanings of take in phrasal verbs 
Take oft en carries a meaning of ‘remove’, as in:
We hope you will take many happy memories away from your stay here.
Phil’s boss has agreed to take him off  the night shift .
Helena took her parents out for a meal. (Unit 42)
Take also sometimes carries a meaning of doing something quickly or something happening 
quickly or abruptly, for example Take it away! (Unit 30), take aback [surprise], take off  (of a 
plane) and take out [kill or destroy in a military battle].

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- intake — n the number of people who begin to study at a school, or who join an organisation at a particular time (units 60)
- take sb aback — if something takes you aback, you are very surprised by it (units 60)
- take along sb/sth or take sb/sth along — to take someone or something with you when you go somewhere (units 60)
- take away sb or take sb away — to take someone with you when you stay somewhere for a short time (units 60)
- take away sth or take sth away — if you take away something [e.g. memory, impression, message] from an event or performance, you remember or think about that thing after the event or performance has finished (units 60)
- take away from sth — to make something seem less good or successful (units 60)
- Take it away! — something that you say in musical contexts to tell someone to start playing or singing (units 30, 60)
- take down sth or take sth down — to remove a large temporary structure from a place by separating it into pieces and taking the pieces away (units 60)
- take off — if an aircraft, bird, or insect takes off, it moves from the ground and begins to fly (units 60)
- take off sth or take sth off — to spend time away from your work (units 60)
- take sb off sth — to stop giving someone a particular type of medical treatment or food (units 60)
- take sb off sth — to stop someone doing a particular task (units 60)
- take-off — n imitation (units 60)
- take out sb or take sb out — to go somewhere and do something with someone, usually something that you have planned or paid for (units 42, 60)
- take out sb/sth or take sb/sth out — to kill someone, or to destroy something (units 60)
- take up sth (with) or take sth up (with) — to discuss something or deal with something (units 60)
- take up sth or take sth up — to use a particular amount of time, space or effort (units 60)
- uptake — n the number of people who have committed themselves to something (units 60)
