In this unit, some phrasal verbs can be grouped by the meaning given by the particle. For 
example, up means uppermost in your mind in summon up, conjure up, call up and stir up. 
Up in store up memories carries a diff erent meaning in that it means gather or collect. Back in 
come back, flood back and flashback carries a sense of a returning memory. Behind and out 
occur with verbs which describe things we don’t want to remember (put something behind 
somebody, block something out / block out something). Grouping the verbs by the meaning 
carried by the particle will help you to remember them.

In each case the play on words depends on the literal meaning of the basic verb in the  
phrasal verb.
1	 To stir up memories means to make memories, often unpleasant ones, appear in your mind.  
A cook stirs ingredients, e.g. the ingredients for a cake or batter for pancakes.
2	 To conjure up memories means to make you remember things from the past. A magician 
traditionally conjures a rabbit out of a hat.
3	 To call up memories means to evoke memories or make someone remember something. Another 
meaning of call up is to require young men to become soldiers.
4	 If a memory sticks with you, then you don’t forget it. Glue is used to stick something to something 
else. The first meaning of stick here is metaphorical and the second is literal.
5	 If memories come flooding back, you suddenly remember a lot of things very clearly. When pipes 
burst – this typically happens if they freeze and then the ice melts – then there will be a flood.
Author’s answers:
1	 I associate my childhood with the colour green because I spent so much time playing in the

4	 I would like to block out the memory of school dinners, particularly of the awful macaroni cheese

5	 I think that music makes memories come back to me more powerfully, although smells can also 
be very potent.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- associate sth with sth — to connect someone or something in your mind with someone or something else (units 2, 18)
- block out sth or block sth out — to stop yourself from thinking about something unpleasant because it upsets you (units 18)
- call up sth or call sth up — if something calls up a memory or an idea, it makes you remember or think about it (units 18)
- come back — if something comes back to you, you remember it (units 18)
- conjure up sth or conjure sth up — to make something [e.g. picture, image, memory] appear in someone’s mind (units 18)
- eat away at sb — if a memory or bad emotion [e.g. bitterness, shame] eats away at someone, they think about it a lot and it makes them very unhappy (units 18)
- flash back — if your mind or thoughts flash back to something that happened to you in the past, you suddenly remember that thing (units 18)
- flashback — n an occasion when you suddenly remember something vividly that happened to you in the past (units 18)
- flood back — if memories flood back, you suddenly remember very clearly a lot of things about an experience or period in the past (units 18)
- put sth behind sb — if you put an unpleasant experience behind you, you forget it so that it does not affect your life (units 18)
- remind sb of sb/sth — to cause someone to think of someone or something (units 18)
- stick with sb — if something sticks with you, you remember it (units 18)
- stir up sth or stir sth up — if something stirs up memories, it makes you remember events in the past, usually ones that make you feel sad (units 18)
- store up sth or store sth up — to remember things, usually so that you can tell people about them later (units 18)
- summon up sth or summon sth up — if something summons up a memory or an image, it makes you remember something or think about something (units 18)
