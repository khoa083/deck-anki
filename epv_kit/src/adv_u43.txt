Feeling tired
There are a lot of phrasal verbs in English relating to the idea of sleep and feeling tired. For example, 
drop off and nod off are both informal and both mean fall asleep, but nod off is used particularly 
about situations when you did not intend to fall asleep.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- break out in/into sth — if you break out in a sweat, it suddenly appears on your skin (units 43)
- build up sb or build sb up — to make someone healthier and stronger after an illness, especially by making sure that they eat a lot (units 43)
- bunged-up — adj slightly informal blocked nose (units 43)
- burn out or burn yourself out — informal to have to stop working because you have become ill or very tired from working too hard (units 43)
- clear up — if an illness clears up, or if medicine clears an illness up, the illness goes away (units 43)
- come off sth — to stop using medicine or drugs (units 43)
- do in sb or do sb in — informal to make someone extremely tired (units 43)
- drop off — to fall asleep (units 2, 43)
- flare up — if a disease that you had before flares up, you suddenly get it again (units 43)
- get over sth — to feel better after having an illness (units 43)
- go around (swh) — if an illness goes around, a lot of people get it (units 43)
- go down — if part of your body that is bigger than usual because of an illness or injury goes down, it starts to return to its usual size (units 43, 58)
- heal up — if a wound heals over, new skin grows over it (units 43)
- knock out sb or knock sb out — to make someone become unconscious or to make someone fall asleep (units 20, 43)
- nod off — informal to fall asleep when you do not intend to go to sleep (units 43)
- pass on sth or pass sth on — to give a disease to another person or animal (units 43)
- pick up sth or pick sth up — to get an infectious illness from someone or something (units 43)
- put sb on sth — to give someone a particular type of medical treatment or food (units 43)
- take sth out of sb — informal to make someone feel very tired (units 43)
- throw off sth or throw sth off — to succeed in getting rid of a slight illness (units 43)
- tire out sb or tire sb out — to make someone very tired (units 43)
- washed out — adj if someone looks washed out, they look tired, pale and ill (units 43)
- wear out sb or wear sb out — to make someone very tired (units 1, 43)
- wipe out sb or wipe sb — out informal to make someone extremely tired (units 43)
