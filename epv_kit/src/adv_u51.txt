How journalists get the news
Some news comes from press releases that are put out by companies or organisations. However, 
journalists also pick up1 stories from their contacts. Occasionally a story gets out2 when a politician or 
film star confides in3 someone who is not discreet. Sometimes a rumour starts and journalists sound out4 
people in the know to check its truth. It may sometimes be difficult for them to root out5 the information 
they want, but journalists get very good at worming stories out of 6 people who don’t want to talk to 
them. Sometimes they may stake out7 their potential subject’s house until they get the story they want. In 
fact, the ability to ferret out8 a story may be as important for a journalist as the ability to write it up well.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- call up sb or call sb up — to order someone to join the army, navy, or airforce (units 51)
- confide in sb — to tell someone things that you keep secret from other people (units 51, 52)
- dumb down sth or dumb sth down — to make something [e.g. textbook, curriculum] simpler and easier to understand (units 51)
- ferret out sth or ferret sth out — to find something after searching for it (units 51)
- get in — if a political party or a politician gets in, they are elected (units 51)
- get on to/onto sth — to be elected as a member of an organisation (units 51)
- get out — if news or information gets out, people hear about it even though someone is trying to keep it secret (units 51)
- give yourself up — to allow the police to catch you (units 51)
- head off sth or head sth off — to prevent a difficult or unpleasant situation from happening (units 51)
- hold out — to continue to defend yourself against an enemy or attack (units 51)
- join in (sth) — to become involved in an activity with other people (units 51)
- join up — to join the army, navy, or airforce (units 51)
- leak out — if secret information leaks out, people who should not know this information find out about it (units 51)
- move (sb) in/into (swh) — to go to a place to deal with a difficult situation (units 51)
- pass off — British & Australian if an event passes off in a good way, it happens in that way (units 51)
- pick up sth or pick sth up — to learn interesting or useful information [e.g. idea, tip, gossip] from someone or something (units 24, 51)
- pull out — to move military troops to a different area (units 51)
- put down sth or put sth down — to use force to stop people opposing the government (units 51)
- put out sth or put sth out — to produce information [e.g. statement, warning, press release] and make it available for everyone to read or hear (units 6, 51, 53)
- root out sth/sb or root sth/sb out — informal to search and find something or someone that is difficult to find (units 51)
- send in sb or send sb in — to send a group of people with special skills [e.g. police, troops] to deal with a difficult situation (units 1, 51)
- shoot down sb/sth or shoot sb/sth down — to destroy an aircraft or make it fall to the ground by firing bullets or weapons at it (units 51)
- sound out sb/sth or sound sb/sth out — to talk to someone in order to discover what they think about an idea or plan (units 45, 51)
- stake out sth or stake sth out — if the police or reporters stake out a building where someone is living or hiding, they watch the building continuously in order to see who is leaving or entering it (units 51)
- walk out — to stop working because of a disagreement with your employer (units 3, 51)
- whip up sth — to try to make people feel strongly about something (units 51)
- worm sth out of sb — to manage to get information from someone which they are trying to keep secret (units 51)
- write up sth or write sth up — to write something on paper or on a computer in a complete or final form, often using notes you have made (units 15, 51)
