Basic meanings of out in phrasal verbs
A very frequent meaning of out is the opposite of in.
I don’t want to leave my job but I suppose they may kick me out.  [(informal) force me to leave]
I’ve had enough of working for such a difficult organisation and I want out. [want to leave] 
Count me out! I’m far too tired to join the skiing trip today. [don’t include me]
Another meaning is when something or part of something extends from its surroundings.
The chalet roof juts out (over its walls) to help prevent snow  
from blocking the doors.  [sticks out beyond the edge]
The rocky peninsula juts out into the bay.
Another meaning is separate.
The woman picked out a kitten to take home.  [chose one  
from a large group] 
We spread our wet clothes out on the grass so they could  
dry in the sunshine.
Another meaning is distribute.
Don’t eat all the sweets yourself, Ollie. Share them out among  
all the children. [give one to each person in the group]

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- clean out sth or clean sth out — to steal everything from a place (units 14)
- count out sb or count sb out — informal to not include someone in an activity (units 14, 23)
- cry out (sth) or cry (sth) out — to suddenly shout something in a loud voice, especially to get someone’s attention (units 14)
- draw out sth or draw sth out — to make something continue for longer than is usual or necessary (units 14)
- drop out — to not do something that you were going to do, or to stop doing something (units 14)
- go out — if something which is burning [e.g. fire] goes out, it stops burning (units 14)
- jut out — to stick out from a surface or beyond the edge of something (units 14)
- kick out sb or kick sb out — to force someone to leave a place or an organisation (units 14)
- lash out — to criticise someone or something in an angry way (units 14)
- pick out sth/sb or pick sth/sb out — to choose one thing or person or several things or people from a large group (units 14)
- scream out (sth) or scream (sth) out — to suddenly shout something in a loud voice, especially to get someone’s attention (units 14)
- share out sth or share sth out — to divide something into smaller amounts and give one amount to each person or thing in a group (units 14)
- shout out (sth) or shout (sth) out — to suddenly shout something in a loud voice, especially to get someone’s attention (units 14)
- sort out sth or sort sth out — to successfully deal with a problem or difficult situation (units 2, 14)
- sort out sth or sort sth out — to arrange or organise things which are untidy (units 14)
- spread out sth or spread sth out — to open something that is folded [e.g. map, towel] and put it down flat on a surface (units 14)
- test out sth or test sth out — to test a theory or new idea by seeing how it works in a practical situation or by finding out what other people think of it (units 14)
- want out — informal to want to leave a place (units 14)
- yell out (sth) or yell (sth) out — to suddenly shout something in a loud voice, especially to get someone’s attention (units 14)
