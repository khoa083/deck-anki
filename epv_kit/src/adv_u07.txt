Possible answers:
1	 If someone warms up an audience, they make it more receptive and friendly, more relaxed 
(perhaps by telling some jokes). Coldness in English equates with unfriendliness and warmth  
with friendliness. Warming up people and warming up food both have the idea of making 
something pleasanter.
2	 If someone falls into a job, they get it very easily and without making any effort. So both falling 
into a hole and falling into a job have the idea of something happening by chance.
3	 If you climb down in an argument, you admit that you were wrong. So both uses of climb down 
suggest moving back to the level of other people from a position in which you were separated 
from others.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- boil down to sth — slightly informal if a situation or problem boils down to a particular thing, that is the main reason for it (units 7)
- brush down/off sb/sth or brush sb/sth down — to use a brush or your hand to remove something (units 7)
- brush off sb/sth or brush sb/sth off — to refuse to listen to what someone says, or to refuse to think about something seriously (units 7)
- climb down — to admit that you are wrong (units 7)
- climb down — if you climb down from a tree, you go back down to the ground (units 7)
- dive in/into sth — to start doing something suddenly without thinking about it (units 7)
- drag sb away from — informal to make somebody stop doing something, so that they can do something else (units 7)
- drink in — to look at, listen to, or experience something with all your attention and to enjoy it very much (units 7)
- eat into sth — to use or take away a large part of something valuable [e.g. savings, profits, leisure time, business] (units 7)
- fish out sth or fish sth out — informal to pull or take something out of a bag or pocket, especially after searching (units 7)
- flood in/into sth — to arrive or enter somewhere in very large numbers or amounts (units 7)
- be glued to sth — to be watching something [esp. television] with all your attention (units 7)
- hit out — to strongly criticise something or someone (units 7)
- nose about/around (swh) — informal to look around a place, often in order to find something (units 7)
- run over — to continue past the expected finishing time (units 7)
- run over sb/sth or run sb/sth over — to hit someone or something with a vehicle and drive over them, injuring or killing them (units 7)
- run over sth — to quickly read something or repeat something in order to remember it or to make sure that it is correct (units 7)
- be sandwiched between sb/sth — informal to be in a small space in the middle of two people or things (units 7)
- shoot up — if the number, amount, or rate of something shoots up, it increases very quickly (units 7)
- soldier on — to continue doing something, although it is difficult or unpleasant (units 7)
- spur on sb or spur sb on — to encourage someone to try harder in order to achieve something (units 7)
- stand by — to do nothing to prevent something unpleasant from happening (units 7)
- strike out — to start doing something that you have not done before (units 7)
- sweep aside sth or sweep sth aside — to refuse to think about something or let it affect your performance (units 7)
- warm up sb or warm sb up — to make a group of people who are going to watch a performance start to enjoy themselves by entertaining them for a short time before the performance (units 7)
- warm up sth or warm sth up — to heat food that has already been cooked (units 7)
