Why is collocation important?
Collocation means the way words combine with one another. When learning a phrasal verb, it 
is important to note what kinds of words the verb is typically used with. For example, can it be 
used for both people and things, or only for people, or only for things? Is it typically used with 
negative things or can it be used for both positive and negative things?  
Look at this chart for the verb pore over [study or look carefully at something] and note how 
the objects it is used with refer especially to books or documents.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- dash off — informal to leave a place quickly (units 5)
- drive off — to leave in a vehicle (units 5)
- ease off/up — to gradually stop or become less (units 5)
- go off sb/sth — to stop liking someone or something (units 5, 41)
- hanker after/for sth — to want something very much, especially something you know you should not want (units 5, 41)
- head off swh — to begin a journey or to leave (units 5)
- hit on sth — to have a good idea, especially one that solves a problem (units 5, 24)
- keep in with sb — British & Australian to continue to be friendly with someone, especially because they can help you (units 5, 59)
- pore over sth — to study or look carefully at something, especially a book or a document (units 5, 32)
- be riddled with sth — be full of something, especially something bad or unpleasant (units 5, 28)
- sail through (sth) — to succeed very easily, especially in a test, examination etc. (units 5, 27, 32)
- stream into swh — to move continuously in one direction, especially if a lot of people do this at the same time (units 5)
