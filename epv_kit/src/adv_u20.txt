Conflict and violence
Metaphorical uses of phrasal verbs to suggest conflict or violence 
The verbs in this group are particularly typical of newspaper language because they have 
metaphorical meanings, which is good for word play.
The government promised to wipe out poverty.  [make it disappear completely]
The minister is fighting off calls for her resignation. 
Some newspapers are trying to drag the prime minister into the scandal.  [talk about or bring 
someone or something into a difficult or unpleasant situation, especially when that person or 
thing is not seen as being connected with the situation]
He published an article in a national newspaper as a way of striking back at his critics.  
[attacking someone who has attacked you (the literal meaning of striking is hitting)]
C
Particles in phrasal verbs connected with conflict and violence
Some of the phrasal verbs in this unit have the particle up (smash up, flare up) where up 
suggests extreme or complete. In come at and fly at at indicates the direction of the violent 
action. Off suggests remove in bump off [(informal), kill] and fight off, and out suggests remove 
completely in wipe out [completely destroy]. Thinking about the meaning the particle adds  
will help you learn the verbs in groups and remember them better.

### MINI DICTIONARY (định nghĩa chính thức của sách cho các mục thuộc unit này):
- break up (sth) or break (sth) up — if a fight breaks up, or if someone breaks it up, the people in it stop fighting or are made to stop fighting (units 20)
- bump sb off or bump off sb — informal to kill someone (units 20)
- bust-up — n informal an angry argument (units 20)
- come at sb — to move towards someone in order to attack them (units 20, 56)
- drag sb/sth into sth — to talk about or bring someone or something into a difficult or unpleasant situation, especially when that person or thing is seen as being connected with the situation (units 20)
- fight off sb/sth or fight sb/sth off — to use violence so that something or someone goes away (units 20)
- flare up — if someone flares up, they suddenly become very angry (units 20)
- fly at sb — to attack someone suddenly and violently (units 20)
- fly into sth — if someone flies into a particular state [e.g. rage, temper, panic] they are suddenly in that state (units 20)
- go back to sth — if a situation goes back to a particular state, it returns to that state (units 20)
- knock out sb or knock sb out — to make someone become unconscious or to make someone fall asleep (units 20, 43)
- push sb about/around/round — to tell someone what to do in a rude or threatening way (units 20)
- push over sb/sth or push sb/sth over — to push someone or something so that they fall to the ground (units 20)
- round on sb — to suddenly turn and attack someone, or shout at them angrily (units 20)
- set (sth/sb) on sb — to attack someone, or to make a person or animal attack someone (units 3, 20)
- smash up sth or smash sth up — to badly damage or destroy something by hitting it many times (units 20)
- strike back (at) — to attack someone who has attacked you (units 1, 20)
- wipe out sth or wipe sth out — to destroy or get rid of something (units 20)
