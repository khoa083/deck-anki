### UNIT 42: too/either / so am I / neither do I etc. (PDF trang 84–85)
### THEORY
Unit
**42**
**too/either so am I / neither do I** etc.
**A**
**too** and **either**
I’m happy.
I’m happy too.
I’m not happy.
I’m not happy either.
We use **too** and **either** at the end of a sentence.
We use **too** after a positive verb:
We use **either** after a negative verb:
A: I’m happy.
A: I’m not happy.
B: I’**m** happy **too**.
B: I**’m not** happy **either**.
A: I enjoyed the film.
A: I can’t cook.
B: I **enjoyed** it **too**.
B: I **can’t either**.
Jane is a doctor. Her husband **is** a
doctor **too**.
Ben doesn’t watch TV. He **doesn’t**
read newspapers **either**.

**so am I** / **neither do I** etc.

So am I.

**so am I** = I am too
**so have I** = I have too (etc.):
**neither am I** = I’m not either
**neither can I** = I can’t either (etc.):
A: **I’m** working.
A: **I haven’t** got a key.
B: **So am I**. (= I’m working too)
A: **I was** late for work today.
B: **Neither have I**. (= I haven’t either)
A: **Kate can’t** cook.
B: **So was Sam**.
A: **I work** in a bank.
B: **Neither can Tom**.
A: **I won’t** (= will not) be here
B: **So do I**.
A: **We went** to the cinema last night.
tomorrow.
B: **Neither will I**.
A: **I never go** to the cinema.
B: Did you? **So did we**.
A: **I’d** like to go to Australia.
B: **Neither do I**.
B: **So would I**.
You can also use **Nor** (= Neither):
A: I’m not married.
B: **Nor am I**. or **Neither am I**.
Remember: So **am I** (not So I am), Neither **have I** (not Neither I have).
